# Public Domain Spanish Books — Easy Vocabulary, Witty Language (research notes)

Raw research notes gathered 2026-09-04. Brief: find public-domain **full novels** in Spanish,
**A2 vocabulary**, **witty** language, weighted toward **Peninsular** Spain, including
**translations into Spanish** where clearly labelled. Explicit exclusion: **no fables, no fairytales**.

## Method and its limits

Project Gutenberg, Internet Archive, Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes and Spanish Wikisource
were all **blocked by this environment's network egress policy** — both `WebFetch` and `curl` returned
403 / EGRESS_BLOCKED. All catalogue facts below were therefore verified through **web search result
metadata** (titles, ebook IDs, release dates, declared language, edition apparatus), not by opening
the texts.

Consequences, stated plainly:

- Ebook IDs, languages and the existence of editorial apparatus (notes/vocabulary) are **verified**.
- Difficulty and wit assessments are **editorial judgement** based on knowledge of these works plus
  the verified edition metadata — they are not derived from a measured readability score.
- **No sample passages were transcribed**, because the texts could not be opened. Nothing in this
  research is quoted verbatim from a primary text.

## Copyright: Spain's 80-year anomaly

Spain applies **life + 80 years** to authors who died **before 7 December 1987**, under the
transitional provisions preserving acquired rights from the 1879 law; **life + 70** applies otherwise.
This is longer than the EU-harmonised norm and it matters for several authors on this list.

| Author | Died | PD in Spain from (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Pedro Antonio de Alarcón | 1891 | ~1972 |
| Juan Valera | 1905 | ~1986 |
| Benito Pérez Galdós | 1920 | ~2001 |
| Emilia Pardo Bazán | 1921 | ~2002 |
| Vicente Blasco Ibáñez | 1928 | ~2009 |
| Armando Palacio Valdés | 1938 | ~2019 |
| Pío Baroja | 1956 | **~2037 — still in copyright in Spain** |

Project Gutenberg is a US host and clears these works on US rules (pre-1930 publication), which is why
Baroja appears there despite remaining under copyright in Spain.

## Audio companions

LibriVox hosts 600–700+ public-domain Spanish audiobooks read by volunteers, including Pardo Bazán,
Pérez Galdós and Alarcón. Catalogue browsable by author, title, genre and language at librivox.org.
Per-title availability for the specific books above was **not individually confirmed** — LibriVox
search results did not surface in this session.
